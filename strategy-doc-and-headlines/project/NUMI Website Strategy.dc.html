01

Website strategy

The job. The site has one job: convert a warm-but-skeptical stranger into a booked discovery call. It is the credibility layer behind every cold call, email, referral, and DM. It is not a brochure, not a portfolio, and not a place to explain how software gets made.

Who it speaks to. Two people, arriving with different sentences in their head. The founder says “I have an idea.” The owner says “my business is getting harder to run.” They share almost nothing in vocabulary — but they share the same anxiety: I don't know what I actually need, and I don't want to be sold an app. That shared anxiety is the site's real audience.

How the two audiences coexist. Not as two landing pages, and not as one blurred message. One shared hero states the promise for both, then a single fork — Which one is you? — lets the visitor self-select in one click. From that point, the page speaks their language: their sentences, their problems, their case studies, their outcome. The fork is a filter, not a detour: both paths end at the same button.

Funnel position. Outbound creates awareness; the site converts it. Because most visitors arrive already having heard the pitch verbally, the site's task is confirmation, not persuasion: this is real, this person is credible, this is what talking to them looks like, here's the button. No form, no questionnaire, no newsletter. Discovery is where information gathering belongs.

What the visitor needs before booking. Five things, in this order: (1) what NUMI is, in one sentence; (2) recognition — “that's my situation”; (3) proof that NUMI won't over-build — the Connect / Enhance / Build idea; (4) evidence of real problems solved and real engineering behind it; (5) a low-stakes next step with a stated price of admission: a conversation, no pitch, no jargon.

Why this structure works. It mirrors the discovery call itself. Recognize the problem → show how you think → show it working → show who you are → talk. A visitor who reads the site is pre-warmed for the call; a visitor who only skims the hero and the fork still gets the two things that matter — what NUMI is, and the button.

02

Information architecture

Recommendation

A hybrid: one app-like shell with fixed navigation, five short views, and case studies as overlay detail views.

Your instinct toward app-like is right, but for a conversion reason rather than a stylistic one. A fixed shell means the CTA is never off screen, and each view is short enough to read completely — so nobody bails mid-scroll and nobody has to hunt for the button. It also makes the site feel like a product NUMI made, which is itself part of the pitch.

Where I diverge from a strict app: each view scrolls if its content needs it, and mobile becomes a single guided flow. Forcing no-scroll would cost legibility on small screens and cramp the case studies, which are the most persuasive content on the site.

Rejected: one long scroll

Reads as an agency template, and buries the fork and the proof under scroll depth.

Rejected: full multi-page

Page loads between five thin pages add friction and lose the compact, intentional feeling.

Chosen: hybrid shell

Instant view transitions, persistent CTA, deep-linkable case studies, honest scrolling where content earns it.

04

Case-study system

One template, five slots, always in the same order: The problem → The approach → What we built → What changed → Capabilities. Technology appears last and as capability language, never as a stack list. Each card is titled by the problem; the project name is the subtitle. That single rule is what stops the section reading like a portfolio.

Clinic intake & scheduling In progress

The flagship story, and the one closest to a small-business prospect's own life. Presented as a before → after comparison of the customer's steps: five disconnected steps on the left, one connected flow on the right. Outcomes stated as intent, not measurement — “fewer steps for patients, less admin for the owner” — with a visible “in progress; measured results to follow” label. Health language stays on workflow and administration; no claims about care or outcomes.

Pitch Portal

The most operationally complex story, so it gets the most room: a fragmented-workflow section (iMessage, Dropbox, email, loose files) resolving into one platform, then the capability list — centralized catalog, metadata on upload, branded pitch rooms, secure expiring links, engagement analytics. This is the story that proves NUMI can hold a real system in its head.

Champagne Therapy

The craft story, deliberately the shortest — a label needed a polished public home for its artists, catalog, and releases; NUMI designed and developed it. Visual-led rather than text-led, and honest about its scope: web design, UX/UI, responsive development.

Enterprise experience Not a client

Visually distinct on purpose — a different card treatment, sitting slightly apart, and explicitly framed as employment: “Before NUMI, Addy built software at Snap Inc.” Three short lines (Snapchat for Web, My AI, Snap Ads / Instant Create) and Sandia. It answers “can they really engineer?” without pretending to be client work.

05

Visual system

Everything below is derived from the existing NUMI logo and decks — same warmth, same roundness, same restraint — with a little more air and motion because the web allows it.

Typography

One rounded geometric sans for display, set very tight and heavy; one warm humanist sans for body at 17–19px. Eyebrow labels in small caps with wide letterspacing, exactly as the decks do. Four sizes total, no more.

Color

Cream ground, cocoa ink, brand peach for surfaces and the mark, terracotta for action and emphasis. Two backgrounds maximum. Color signals; it never decorates. No gradients beyond the logo's own soft shadow.

Shape & radius

Three radii: 999px for pills and buttons, 28px for cards, 40px for large panels. Nothing sharp anywhere. Shapes are pill and circle only — the logo's own vocabulary.

Buttons & cards

One primary button: terracotta fill, white text, soft offset shadow, pressing down 1px on click. One secondary: outlined pill. Cards are white on cream with a hairline warm border, no drop shadows except on hover.

Spacing

An 8px scale, used generously: 24 inside cards, 48 between blocks, 96 between views. Whitespace is the main luxury signal here — density would read as agency.

Icons & graphics

No icon set, no illustrations of technology. Meaning comes from type, pills, and the logo's own smile motif. Project imagery is real screens; until you send them, striped placeholders labeled with what belongs there.

Motion

One easing curve, 180–320ms. Views cross-fade and lift 8px. Cards lift 2px on hover. The logo's smile is the only continuous movement, and it's slow. Everything respects reduced-motion.

Navigation

A floating rounded bar: mark on the left, five text labels centered with a sliding pill on the active one, terracotta CTA on the right. On mobile it becomes a bottom bar with the CTA always visible.

06

Conversion strategy

Where the CTA lives. Permanently in the nav, once in the hero, once at the end of each expanded situation card, once at the end of every case study, and once in the closing block. That's frequent but never stacked — the visitor meets it exactly when a question has just been answered.

Calendly. Straight to your booking page in a new tab, so the site stays open behind it. No embedded widget on first contact: an inline calendar makes the page slower and heavier, and on mobile it's worse than the native Calendly flow. If you'd rather keep people on-site, the alternative is a full-screen rounded overlay with the embed inside — a one-line change once you send the link.

Supporting cold outreach. Two things make the site work after a phone call: the hero repeats your verbal pitch almost word for word, and the situation cards repeat what the prospect just told you. Recognition is what converts here, not novelty.

Trust without fake proof. No logos, no testimonials, no invented numbers. Credibility instead comes from specificity — the fragmented-clinic story is unfakeably real — plus verifiable credentials (NYU, Snap, Sandia), the visible quality of the site itself, and the honesty of labeling work “in progress.” Admitting what isn't measured yet buys more trust than a fabricated percentage.

How case studies convert. Each one is a mirror. A clinic owner recognizes the intake mess; a founder recognizes the “I have designs, not a developer” problem; a bigger prospect recognizes the systems sprawl. The CTA at the end of a story converts far better than one in a vacuum, because the visitor has just watched their own problem get solved.

07

Responsive experience

Desktop. The full app shell. Floating top nav, two-column hero, side-by-side situation cards, case studies as overlay detail views, process on a horizontal track. Views fit the viewport with minimal scrolling.

Tablet. Same shell, single-column hero with the mark above the type, situation cards still side by side but taller, process wrapping to two rows. Touch targets go to 48px and hover effects become tap states.

Mobile. Not a collapsed desktop. Navigation moves to a bottom bar with the five views behind a compact menu and the CTA always visible above the thumb. Each view becomes one short, well-spaced screen — one idea per scroll length, big type, no paragraph over three lines. Case studies become full-screen sheets that slide up. The type scale drops one step; the spacing scale does not, so the site stays airy rather than cramped.

Five views, one button, four honest stories. It's the strongest version of NUMI's site right now because it's sized to what's true today: a new company with real engineering behind it, one flagship project underway, and a founder doing outbound. It gives a prospect everything needed to say yes to a conversation in about ninety seconds, and nothing to get lost in.

It also grows cleanly. New case studies drop into the same template. If a service line ever needs its own page, the shell already supports deep views. And when the clinic project finishes, the placeholder outcomes become real numbers in one place, without redesigning anything.
